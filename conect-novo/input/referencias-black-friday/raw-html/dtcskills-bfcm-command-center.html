What this skill does

The Stack's seasonal conductor for Black Friday / Cyber Monday. Three modes in one folder (campaigns/bfcm-YYYY/). Plan builds seven files plus calendar.csv: (1) offer math per SKU at every discount depth and for each bundle candidate - contribution, break-even ROAS, target ROAS, allowable CPA - with the discount cap enforced from guardrails.md and a promo P&L with labeled assumptions; (2) a day-by-day email and SMS calendar from Nov 17 through Dec 7 by segment with a suppression rule on every send and the honesty rule on extensions; (3) the pause / modify / add table for every active flow with effective and revert dates and the modified copy; (4) creative briefs by phase with kill CPAs from the offer table; (5) the BFCM landing page outline, announcement bar copy, PDP promo modules, shipping-threshold arithmetic, and a 20-point readiness checklist scored 0-100; (6) the post-BFCM cohort plan. Execute is read-only: a run sheet per day and a pacing tracker fed by Shopify, Klaviyo, and Polar or Kleio reads that flags any day under 85% of plan and names the next lever. Debrief is append-only: graded winners and losers to proven_winners.md and a BFCM actuals block to offers_and_pricing.md. Chains the Email Campaign Engine, Email Flow Architect, DTC Ad Creative, Static Ad Prompt Engine, Social & UGC Engine, Landing Page Copy System, CRO Audit, and Retention Optimizer inline when installed. Never writes to an external system. Beta through BFCM 2026.

The Problem

BFCM gets planned across six tools, four people, and a spreadsheet nobody trusts - and the offer gets picked by gut.

Most brands run their biggest week of the year without ever computing what a discount does to contribution. At 25% off a $48 consumable, break-even ROAS is 3.07x; most paid accounts do not clear that, so the sale makes revenue and loses money. Then the welcome offer stacks with the sale code, "ends tonight" gets extended on Monday, the last-call email goes to people who bought that morning, and nobody writes down what happened before January. The pieces exist across the Stack - campaigns, flows, creative, pages, retention - but nothing holds them to one offer, one set of dates, and one plan.

Offer decision
Without this skill

""We always do 25% off sitewide." No margin math, no bundle comparison, no inventory check. Last year Citrus sold out on Sunday and 212 orders stacked the welcome code with the sale code."

With this skill

"A scenario table at every discount depth for the single and the 2-pack: contribution, break-even ROAS, target ROAS, allowable CPA. Bundle-led headline (Two Tubs $72: break-even 2.31x vs 3.07x for a single at the same 25%), a refusal of the 40% request that exceeds the guardrail cap, a promo P&L with every assumption labeled, and the sell-out day if the plan would hit one."

The week itself
Without this skill

With this skill

"A run sheet per day with a morning checklist, a pacing tracker fed by Shopify and Polar or Kleio reads that flags any day under 85% of plan the same evening and names the next lever from the offer table, and a Dec 2 revert checklist for every flow change."

What's Inside

What you get.

01

Layer 1: Offer math

Contribution, break-even ROAS, target ROAS, and allowable CPA for every SKU at 0-40% off and for each bundle candidate, using the same model as the free Break-even ROAS calculator. Enforces the discount cap in your guardrails.md and refuses offers beyond it, with the file quoted.

02

Layer 2: Day-by-day calendar + CSV

Nov 17 through Dec 7 with email and SMS by segment (VIP, Engaged, Lapsed, New, SMS, BFCM Purchasers), two-per-day and three-SMS-per-window caps, the honesty rule on extensions, and a suppression rule on every single send. Ships as 02-calendar.md and calendar.csv.

03

Layer 3: Flow changes

The pause / modify / add table for every active flow with effective and revert dates - the welcome-offer collision, cart timing, post-purchase holds, review-request delays - plus the modified message copy.

04

Layers 4-5: Creative and site

Briefs by phase (tease, early access, live, last call, after) with kill CPAs from the offer table; the BFCM landing page outline, announcement bar copy per phase, PDP promo modules, the shipping-threshold arithmetic, and a 20-point readiness checklist scored 0-100.

05

Layer 6: After

Quiet period, the Dec 8 send, second-purchase and review timing dated from delivery, subscription conversion at the standing offer, cohort targets, and what to measure in January.

06

Execute and debrief modes

Read-only run sheet and pacing tracker during the window; an append-only debrief that writes graded winners and losers to proven_winners.md and a "BFCM actuals" block to offers_and_pricing.md so next year starts from this year.

FAQ

Common questions.

How is this different from the Email Campaign Engine's BFCM framework?

The Campaign Engine owns the email craft: the 5-7 email sequence, segment matrix, and copy. This skill sits above it and decides the offer with margin math, sets the dates, adds SMS, flows, creative, site prep, and the after-plan, and holds all of them to one plan. When you run /bfcm, it calls the Campaign Engine for Layer 2 in the same session.

Does it send anything or create discount codes?

No. Every mode is read-only against Shopify, Klaviyo, and your analytics. It produces the plan files, the run sheet, and the pacing tracker; you do the sending in Klaviyo. It never writes to an external system.

What if this is our first BFCM?

The no-history path builds the P&L from your engaged list and ad budget instead of last year, labels every projected number as an estimate, and skips every year-over-year comparison. The debrief then records this year so next year has history.

Why is it labeled beta?

This is the skill's first BFCM. The frameworks it chains are mature; the way it wires them together for one event is new, and the pacing day-share defaults are starting splits rather than measured ones. The Beta Notes section in the skill says exactly what is untested and where to report problems. Fixes ship through /dtc-upgrade.
