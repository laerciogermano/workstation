AI Novel Writing Prompts for Every Stage of a Chapter

Copy five focused prompts for chapter planning, scene drafting, continuity review, revision, and updating your story notes.

Useful AI novel writing prompts change with the stage of the chapter. Planning needs alternatives; drafting needs boundaries; review needs evidence. The five prompts below keep those jobs separate. Replace every bracketed field with your own material and keep accepted facts outside the chat as your source of truth.

Prompt 1: Plan a chapter around a change

Help me plan chapter [number] of [story].
Starting state: [what is true at the opening]
Ending change: [what must be different]
Viewpoint and current knowledge: [character + limits]
Relevant canon: [approved facts]
Propose 3 possible scene sequences, each with goal, obstacle,
decision, and consequence. Explain the tradeoff of each sequence.
Do not draft prose or add new world rules.

Use this before committing to a sequence. Compare how the alternatives create the same change through different choices. If all three rely on a new secret power, revise the constraints before asking for more detail.

Prompt 2: Draft one scene

Draft the selected scene in [viewpoint, tense].
Scene goal: [goal]
Conflict: [obstacle]
Turn: [decision or discovery]
Current character state: [knowledge, possessions, injuries]
Voice notes: [concrete preferences and an excerpt of my own prose]
Allowed invention: [minor details]
Stop after [specific ending beat].
List any new facts you introduced after the scene.

A specific stopping point prevents the assistant from resolving the next chapter too early. Use your own prose as the voice reference and describe its features rather than requesting an imitation of another writer.

Prompt 3: Check continuity with evidence

Audit this draft against the approved context below.
Return a table: draft quotation, conflicting fact, source location,
severity, and smallest possible repair.
Check viewpoint knowledge, chronology, injuries, objects, and motives.
Separate definite conflicts from questions that need author judgment.
Do not rewrite the chapter yet.

An audit without quotations can be hard to verify. Reject issues that cannot be traced to the draft or notes. The assistant can miss contradictions and can also flag intentional ambiguity as an error.

Prompt 4: Revise one diagnosed problem

Revise only [scene or passage] to address [specific issue].
Preserve [plot outcome, viewpoint, facts, lines to keep].
Change [permitted elements].
Return the revision and a short explanation of what changed.
Do not introduce a new character, ability, or off-page event.

For example, ask to make Mara's decision to leave the tower more reluctant while preserving the destination and the key's owner. This is easier to assess than an instruction to make the entire chapter “more emotional.”

Prompt 5: Propose the next context update

Using only the accepted chapter, propose updates to the story bible.
For each update include: field, previous state, new state,
supporting quotation, and whether it is fact or inference.
Also list unresolved promises carried into the next chapter.
Do not treat rejected drafts or future outline events as canon.

Review the proposed updates before using them. Keep a versioned copy of the previous notes so that a mistaken update can be reversed.

Run a small rehearsal

Try the sequence on a single scene before using it for a novel. Save the prompt, input context, generated draft, and your accepted revision. That gives you a repeatable record of what helped and what still needed editing. Download the prompt pack or follow the complete chapter workflow for the order of operations.
